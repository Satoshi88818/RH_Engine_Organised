# Fake off-line zero test

See the original notes for full tables and interpretation.

This experiment inserts a hypothetical off-line zero quadruple (\(½ \pm \delta \pm i\gamma_0\)) into the zero set used by the candidate energy \(E(\sigma)\) and measures:

- whether the minimum of \(E\) moves,
- the location and size of the resulting kinks in \(E''\),
- the change \(\Delta E\).

**Main findings (with the concrete \(E\) defined in this package):**

1. The minimum stays at \(\sigma = 1/2\).
2. Each displaced zero produces a kink whose integrated mass matches \(2\pi/L\) to high accuracy (Littlewood).
3. \(\Delta E\) is finite and mostly a normalisation shift; there is no “infinite action”.
4. A zero outside the analysis window is invisible to \(E\).

Consequently this particular energy functional cannot distinguish RH from its negation; the V-shape at \(1/2\) is forced by the functional equation and the symmetry of the inserted quadruple.
